# Module 1: How modern AI works, and where it fails

Status: **researched**, ready to start. Weeks 1–3. About 6 core hours a week.

## Why this module comes first

You already use Claude well enough to get useful answers. This module gives you the model *behind* the answers: you'll be able to predict where Claude will be strong, where it will go wrong, and what to check before relying on it. Every later module builds on that.

The book club business plan runs alongside from day one. Each week you use the new idea on your own business, so by week 3 you have a business snapshot, a set of customer hypotheses and a fact-checked list of comparable clubs and podcasts.

## By the end of week 3 you'll be able to

1. Explain in plain words how an LLM turns your prompt into an answer.
2. Explain how a raw text-predictor becomes a helpful assistant, and what that process does and doesn't give it.
3. Look at a prompt and predict whether Claude is likely to get it wrong, and why.
4. Fact-check AI output quickly with a checklist you wrote yourself.
5. Explain why an impressive demo or benchmark score doesn't prove a tool will work reliably for *your* tasks.
6. Choose sensible privacy settings and know what not to paste into a chat.

## How a week works

- **Learn** (~1.5–2.5 h): a short explanation in the week's file plus one or two videos.
- **Try** (~1.5–2 h): guided experiments in Claude, described step by step.
- **Build** (~1.5–3 h): your piece of the book club plan.
- **Check-in** (~30 min): send me what you made. We look at it together, I give feedback, and I adjust the next week. No marks, no tests.

Optional: [flashcards.csv](flashcards.csv) holds about 25 question-and-answer cards for any flashcard app that imports CSV, such as Anki. Five minutes every few days makes the concepts stick far better than rereading (see S11 in the source register).
